# Angular cancellation and the original Fourier window in the lower free-norm range

For the original all-s literal-unit component of the half-damped, positive-auxiliary observation, retain all finite compensation assignments, the original/core nonflat markers, the complete fixed source and the complete old Gaussian-T restorer. Let d be the free squarefree ideal in c=E_c d and A_(gamma,lambda,d) its actual coefficient after extracting mu(d), as defined below. The normalized lower-range amplitude satisfies, for every fixed epsilon>0,

    sum_(gamma,lambda,Nd<=Z^b) Z^(3/16)|A_(gamma,lambda,d)|
             <<_(q,b,epsilon) Z^(Psi_F(b)+epsilon),
    Psi_F(b)=b-11/48,             3/16<=b<=31/96,
    Psi_F(b)=-1/10+3b/5,          31/96<=b<=1.

In particular the full lower range Nd<=Z^(11/48) costs O_(q,epsilon)(Z^epsilon), while the cutoff Nd<=sqrt(Z) costs O_(q,epsilon)(Z^(1/5+epsilon)), improving the earlier absolute-r price Z^(1/4+epsilon). The original Fourier decay already paid every fixed range Nd<=Z^(3/16-eta) by arbitrarily negative powers. Thus the weaker angular-only endpoint 1/6 is not advertised as a new low-range extension.

The new ingredients are cancellation of the prescribed type-minus-six angular r sum with its genuine moving holes, a logarithmic bound for the critical restoring series, and optimization retaining the original r-independent Fourier decay. The estimates are unconditional and use only fixed-field lattice/ideal counting and the original smooth profiles. They provide no estimate for the central Nd comparable to Z Mobius amplitude, its mixed energy with the paid range, or an arbitrary r-dependent or pre-projection physical h/w mask. They do not give a new spectral gap, zeta-zero exclusion or RH proof.

## Part I. The prescribed angular-r sum, true holes and complete restoring operator

### 1. A smooth fixed-ray angular sum has no zero Poisson frequency

Work in the original Eisenstein field, with its fixed primary/S0 conventions. The angular factor kappa((rho))=(conjugate(rho)/|rho|)^6 is independent of the choice of unit associate. Let psi be any of the fixed finite multiplicative characters obtained by expanding the original ray indicator at r^6. It has no growing modulus and is invariant under the six unit associates in this occurrence. Fixed bad-prime zero extensions remain in psi or in the fixed periodic lattice weight.

For R>0 put

 S_psi(R)=sum_w kappa(w)psi(w)(Nw)^-3
                              V_G((Nw/R)^6).               (1)

Then for every fixed K>0,

 |S_psi(R)|<=C_K R^-3 min(R^K,R^-K).                        (2)

To prove the large-R estimate, write the sum over generators as one sixth of a fixed finite linear combination of sums over lattice cosets. The smooth scaled function is

 F(z)=(conjugate(z)/|z|)^6 |z|^-6 V_G(|z|^12),

extended by zero at the origin. The actual log-Gaussian makes F Schwartz and flat at zero. Its integral over the complex plane is zero by its nonzero angular type. Under z=rho/sqrt(R), (1) is R^-3 times these lattice sums. Poisson summation gives a zero term proportional to R integral F, hence zero, and nonzero dual frequencies of size at least a fixed constant times sqrt(R). Arbitrary Schwartz decay proves (2) for R>=1. The periodic ray weights and their fixed finite Fourier expansions change only the constants. For R<=1, the elementary bound V_G(y)<=C_A y^-A and the absolutely convergent ideal sum prove arbitrarily high positive powers, giving the other half of (2). No prime distribution input or zero-free region is used.

### 2. True totient and moving r holes

Let Q,B be finite ideals, containing only the actual variable hole Q and the primes excluded from the totient product, respectively. Fixed bad support is unchanged. Consider

 T_(Q,B)(R)=sum_((v,Q)=1) kappa(v)psi(v)(Nv)^-3
       V_G((Nv/R)^6) product_(p|v,p not dividing B)(1-1/Np).

For every fixed A>0,

 |T_(Q,B)(R)|<=C_A tau(rad Q) min(R^-3,R^A),             (3)

uniformly in B,Q. In the small-R part the divisor factor is unnecessary, but the displayed common bound is convenient.

Indeed first expand the totient as sum_(d|v,(d,B)=1)mu(d)/Nd. Because (v,Q)=1, restrict d to (d,QB)=1 before applying inclusion-exclusion to the remaining factor. For e|rad Q one now has (d,e)=1, and the actual imposed divisor is de, not an uncontrolled least common multiple. Multiplicativity gives an exact sum of terms

 mu(d)mu(e) kappa(de)psi(de) (Nd)^-4(Ne)^-3
                                      S_psi(R/(Ne Nd)).

Each fixed e contributes at most C R^-3, by (2) and

 sum_d (Nd)^-1 min((T/Nd)^K,(T/Nd)^-K)<=C_K,
                                      T=R/Ne>0.             (4)

For T>=1, (4) follows by dyadic ideal counting and a convergent geometric sum on either side of Nd comparable to T. For T<1 use the convergent sum T^K sum_d(Nd)^(-1-K). Summing e proves the large-R bound. The original summand has totient and hole factors of modulus at most one, so the small-R bound follows directly before these expansions. Every exchange is absolutely convergent on the original weighted sum.

Apply (3) to the actual unit-column r sum. Let E_r be the marked-r slot product, with E_r|r and (E_r,c)=1. Write r=E_r v; do NOT impose (v,E_r)=1. If (E_r,t)!=1 the original term vanishes. Otherwise the only moving r-unit hole is (v,t)=1. In phi(rad(gr) outside c1s1)/N, primes already in g or E_r supply fixed factors of modulus at most one; the remaining totient expansion excludes the finite primes of g E_r c1 s1. The ray depends on r through its sixth power and thus supplies precisely one of the fixed psi in (1). The original z sum, W1 and Fourier window have no further r-dependence.

Consequently, with V=T exp(-alpha)/(Nd (NE_r)^5), the complete true r sum is bounded by

 C_A tau(rad t) (NE_r)^-3 H_A(V),
                  H_A(V)=min(V^-1/2,V^A).                   (5)

This includes the original factor kappa(E_r) and all actual r/g overlaps. Taking absolute values over r BEFORE its smooth angular cancellation would only give the older V^-1/3 bound; (5) uses its exact prescribed phase.

### 3. The critical restoring denominator costs a logarithm

The old T-restorer is not bounded by simply applying its geometric series to the single power T^-1/2: that would have ratio one. Its full two-sided envelope must be retained. If Q_aux=Nq_aux and A>0, then

 sum_(j>=0) Q_aux^-3j H_A(V/Q_aux^(6j))
 <=C_A H_A(V)[1+log_+ V/log Q_aux].                       (6)

For V>=1, split at j=floor(log V/(6 log Q_aux)). All preceding terms equal V^-1/2; the small-V terms beyond it form an absolutely convergent geometric tail of the same size. For V<1 the whole sum is bounded by C_A V^A. The two nonidentity numerator terms have the original coefficients Q_aux^-5/6 and Q_aux^-4 and scale reductions Q_aux and Q_aux^6. On the large-V envelope their prices are Q_aux^-1/3 and Q_aux^-1, respectively; on the small-V envelope they are smaller. Thus the COMPLETE numerator and denominator obey the same logarithmic bound.

Here V<=T exp(-alpha), with ambient T=Z. The logarithm costs at most a fixed multiple of 1+log Z+|alpha|. Every fixed exponential source moment is available. It can therefore be absorbed into Z^epsilon after the original full source integration. No infinite critical geometric series has been summed as a convergent constant.

### 4. A true positive majorant after the r sum

Use the exact coefficient A_(gamma,lambda,d) of the original all-s unit column, with c=E_c d and one compensation assignment (D,E_c,E_r). Insert (5) BEFORE the positive z count, s1-annulus count and powerful-t sum in (U1). This gives

 |A_(gamma,lambda,d)|
 <= C_A |W_lambda| A_T(Z)^-1
   (ND)^(-11/6)(NE_c)^(-5/6) X^(1/3)(Nd)^(-5/6)
   (NE_r)^-3 (Nc)^epsilon
   integral |f_q(alpha)|exp(-alpha/3)
        H_A(T exp(-alpha)/(Nd (NE_r)^5))
        [1+log Z+|alpha|] d alpha.                         (7)

Here is the full exterior calculation. The positive z count contributes

 (ND NE_c Nd)^(1/6) X^(-1/6)exp(alpha/6)/(Ng).

Multiplying 1/Nc1=Ng/(NE_c Nd) and the outside sqrt(X)exp(-alpha/2)/(Y ND) gives X^(1/3)exp(-alpha/3)D^(-5/6)E_c^(-5/6)(Nd)^(-5/6)/Y. The actual s1 annulus has at most C Y/(ND Nt Ng) ideals; if its upper endpoint is below the first nonzero norm it is empty. This proves the displayed D^(-11/6) and leaves the g divisor reciprocal. Thus the claimed count uses the actual annular support, including subunit scales, and no unbounded s1 multiplicity.

The extra tau(rad t) in (5) is summable in the original powerful-t sum with weight (Nt)^-1. All finite sectors cost only their fixed number. The small divisor powers from g and the actual holes are absorbed by fixed divisor bounds. Activation is the unchanged simultaneous X,T shift, hence exp(-alpha/3) here is the true z-count prefactor just computed. The old restorer has been included using (6).

For fixed source q, the alpha integral in (7) is bounded by a fixed moment times H_A(T/(Nd (NE_r)^5)), with the displayed logarithm absorbed into a small Z power. Let V0=T/(NE_r)^5. Ordinary ideal counting, splitting at Nd=V0, now gives for B>=1 and A>1/6

 sum_(Nd<=B) (Nd)^(-5/6)(NE_r)^-3 H_A(V0/Nd)
 <= C_A T^-1/2 (NE_r)^-1/2
                         min(B^(2/3), V0^(2/3)).             (8)

Small divisor powers only modify the exponents by epsilon. Formula (8) is valid also for V0<1, using the small-V bound and A>1/6. In particular the marked-r price is now (NE_r)^-1/2 on the large-V part, not the older (NE_r)^-4/3. Its window restriction V0 is essential and has not been discarded.

### 5. Full slot optimization and the improved lower range

Write e=log_Z(NE_r) and e_D=log_Z(ND) for the original disjoint slot assignments, up to their fixed compact support constants. Thus e,e_D>=0 and e+e_D<=ell=1/6. After summing each actual prime pool in l1, the E_c, E_r, D slot prices in (8) are respectively

 P_i^(1/6), P_i^(1/2), P_i^(-5/6).

Multiplying the original normalization Z^(3/16), X^(1/3)T^-1/2 and all slot prices gives

 sum_(gamma,lambda,Nd<=B) Z^(3/16)|A_(gamma,lambda,d)|
 <= C_(q,epsilon) Z^epsilon
   max_(0<=e<=1/6) Z^(-1/6+e/3)
          min(B^(2/3),Z^((2/3)(1-5e))).                    (9)

Assignments with e_D>0 carry the additional factor Z^-e_D and can only improve this upper bound. Fixed support ratios, the finite number of assignments and A_T^-1 are paid by the same small Z powers as in the original slot proof. No prime phase is averaged here: the r cancellation has already been performed in each original coefficient before the positive sums.

For B=Z^b with 0<=b<=1, maximizing the two affine exponents in (9) gives

 Psi(b)= -1/9+2b/3,                 0<=b<=1/6,
 Psi(b)= -1/10+3b/5,               1/6<=b<=1,

and the right side is O_(q,epsilon)(Z^(Psi(b)+epsilon)). In the second interval the maximum occurs at e=(1-b)/5; in the first it is bounded by the endpoint e=1/6. The actual finite set of assignments may be smaller, which only improves the bound.

In particular the entire free-norm range Nd<=Z^(1/6) costs O_(q,epsilon)(Z^epsilon), with every original compensation assignment and finite sector retained. This is not a first improvement of that very low range: the original Fourier window already makes Nd<=Z^(3/16-eta) arbitrarily small for each fixed eta>0, since D Ec<=C Z^(1/6). The genuine new price from the present angular calculation is the following larger-cutoff bound. At B=Z^(1/2), the price is Z^(1/5+epsilon), improving the previous Z^(1/4+epsilon) bound. For any fixed desired amplitude rate d>0 with 1/6+(5/3)d<1, a buffered range

 Nd<=Z^(1/6+(5/3)d-eta)

costs O(Z^(d-c_eta)) after choosing the preliminary small exponents sufficiently small. This is a legitimate lower-range payment by the triangle inequality, not a bound on its cross energy with an unknown complementary amplitude.

For the cutoff B=Z^((1+delta)/2), fixed 0<delta<=1, the same proved inequality gives Z^(1/5+3delta/10+epsilon). This is only a lower-range amplitude estimate. No cancellation in the remaining large-free-norm Mobius kernel or its mixed energy is inferred.


## Part II. The joint Fourier-angular lower-range bound

### 1. The stronger original z-count

For every fixed J>=0 and every A>0, ideal counting and Schwartz decay give

 sum_z |Wtilde0(A (Nz)^6)|
                  <=C_J A^-1/6 (1+A)^-J.                  (1)

For A<=1 use the usual dyadic ideal count. For A>=1 use a Schwartz power larger than J+1/6 and an absolutely convergent ideal sum. The same fixed primary and bad conventions only change the constant. No r variable occurs in this Fourier window.

For a genuine compensation label and free ideal d, the actual argument is

 A=X exp(-alpha) (Ng)^6/(ND NE_c Nd).

Thus (1) is the old z-count multiplied by the additional uniform factor

             (1+X exp(-alpha)/(ND NE_c Nd))^-J.            (2)

The g^-1 factor of the old count remains intact. First sum the prescribed angular r phase as in Part I, then apply (1) with all original z holes dropped only in this positive majorant. The two steps do not interfere: the actual z support and Fourier window are r-independent, while the totient-r expansion was already estimated uniformly in g,c1,s1.

After the original source integration, (2) contributes

                     (1+X/(ND NE_c Nd))^-J                (3)

up to a fixed source moment. Indeed each shift in (2) is bounded by exp(J|alpha|) times (3); the two-sided angular envelope and its restored logarithm cost only additional fixed exponential/polynomial source moments. The complete T-only restorer does not change X or the Fourier argument, so (3) also remains in every restoring term.

### 2. All slots and the constrained exponent

Let S_slots be the product of the norms of all original selected primes. It is comparable to Z^(1/6), and exactly

             ND NE_c NE_r=S_slots.

Consequently X/(ND NE_c B) is comparable to Z^(3/16) NE_r/B. Since (3) is increasing with Nd, every lower-range sum Nd<=B retains the common factor (1+X/(ND NE_c B))^-J. The independently derived angular-r bound for each assignment therefore improves to

 Z^epsilon Z^(-1/6+e/3-e_D)
  min(B^(2/3),Z^((2/3)(1-5e)))
              (1+Z^(3/16+e)/B)^-J,                       (4)

with e=log_Z NE_r, e_D=log_Z ND and fixed compact-support constants understood. The same finite slot counting and normalization as in Part I apply. No new condition was imposed on the actual sums.

For B=Z^b and any fixed small epsilon>0, choose a small buffer epsilon1 and then a fixed sufficiently large Schwartz exponent J. Assignments with e>b-3/16+epsilon1 are arbitrarily small powers compared with the claimed bound in (4). On the remaining assignments one has e<=b-3/16+epsilon1. This is an upper-bound split using (4), not a deletion of a transition region without error.

The unconstrained angular optimum is e=(1-b)/5. It is compatible with e<=b-3/16 exactly when b>=31/96. Thus, after absorbing the buffer in epsilon,

 sum_(gamma,lambda,Nd<=Z^b) Z^(3/16)|A_(gamma,lambda,d)|
  <<_(q,b,epsilon) Z^(Psi_F(b)+epsilon),

 Psi_F(b)=b-11/48,                  3/16<=b<=31/96,
 Psi_F(b)=-1/10+3b/5,               31/96<=b<=1.             (5)

At b=31/96 both expressions equal3/32. In the first range the maximum of the increasing left branch occurs at e=b-3/16, giving exactly b-11/48. Assignments with D slots only improve it through Z^-e_D. For every fixed b<3/16, the already available Fourier bound alone gives an arbitrary negative power of Z, with constants depending on the gap3/16-b. This last fact is recorded to avoid advertising the weaker b=1/6 consequence of the angular-only calculation as new.

### 3. Consequence for the actual middle amplitude

Equation (5) pays the entire original free-norm range Nd<=Z^(11/48) at O_(q,epsilon)(Z^epsilon), with all original compensation assignments, the fixed sector vector, full source and old restorer retained. For a fixed desired amplitude growth rate0<d<=3/32, and a sufficiently small fixed positive eta, the range

                  Nd<=Z^(11/48+d-eta)                     (6)

costs O(Z^(d-eta/2)) after selecting the preliminary small exponents. If the endpoint in (6) falls below3/16, the stronger old Fourier estimate applies instead. This is a triangle-inequality lower-range payment. It does not claim that its cross term with an unestimated middle is already small in the required one-sided energy.

For the cutoff B=Z^((1+delta)/2), fixed 0<delta<=1, the second branch in (5) gives Z^(1/5+3delta/10+epsilon). The actual upper free-norm Gaussian tail is unchanged. Only the lower endpoint of the remaining free-norm middle is paid here; the central Nd comparable to Z amplitude and the whole original signed Mobius correlation remain unestimated.
