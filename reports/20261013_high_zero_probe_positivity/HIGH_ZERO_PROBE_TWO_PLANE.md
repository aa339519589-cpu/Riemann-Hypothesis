# Full-operator high-ordinate positivity on individual zeta-zero dual-jet sectors and complete conjugate-probe planes

Date: 2026-10-13. Final target remains an unconditional proof that **every nontrivial zero of ζ has real part exactly 1/2**.

This theorem studies precisely the zeta-zero dual probes newly constructed in [ZETA_ZERO_DUAL_PROBES.md](../20261013_theta_zero_dual_complement/ZETA_ZERO_DUAL_PROBES.md), **inside the original full Gamma / all-prime operator**, rather than a truncated or replaced model. It proves a uniform high-ordinate *positive* Rayleigh asymptotic for every fixed-multiplicity zero-jet sector and, crucially, for the **entire real two-plane** generated by an off-line zero together with its conjugate, uniformly even as that conjugate pair coalesces toward the critical line.

This identifies a genuine failure mechanism for a proposed quick RH closure: individual high-zero dual probes and their complete conjugate planes do **not** yield a negative direction merely because the underlying zero lies off the line. The unresolved global complementary sign remains a many-vector problem.

**No complete unconditional RH proof is claimed or obtained. No ζ zero proportion, auxiliary spectral gap, or changed target is substituted.**

## 1. Unchanged theta source, form and dual family

Work on the original even physical \(L^2(\mathbb R,dx)\):
\[
\Phi(x)=\sum_{n\ge1}(4\pi^2 n^4e^{9x/2}-6\pi n^2e^{5x/2})
e^{-\pi n^2e^{2x}},\quad
h(x)=\cosh(x/2),\quad
m(x)^2=\frac{\Phi(x)}{2h(x)}.
\]
The complete theta is strictly positive, even and doubly exponentially decreasing, together with derivatives. In particular \(m\) and \(m^2\), even after multiplication by \(e^{\pm |x|/2}\) and any fixed polynomial in \(x\), belong to Schwartz space.

Keep the exact original signed selfadjoint operator
\[
\boxed{H=M_m b(D)M_m-V,\quad
b(t)=\Re\psi(1/4+it/2)-\log\pi+C_0\ge0,\quad
V=C_0M_{m^2}+
\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}
 M_m(\tau_{\log n}+\tau_{-\log n})M_m.}\tag{1}
\]
Here \(C_0=\gamma_{\rm Euler}+\pi/2+3\log2+\log\pi\). All prime powers occur in the norm-convergent bounded selfadjoint series \(V\). The original maximal form domain is retained. The exact Gamma symbol obeys
\[
b(t)=\log(2+|t|)+O(1),\qquad b(t)\asymp\log(2+|t|)
\quad (|t|\to\infty).
\tag{2}
\]
Only the standard digamma asymptotic is used; there is no finite-Gamma cutoff.

For \(z\in\mathbb C\), define
\[
\psi_z(x)=m(x)\cos(zx),\qquad
\psi_z^{(j)}(x)=m(x)x^j\cos(zx+j\pi/2).
\tag{3}
\]
Every one of these vectors lies in the **actual operator domain** of \(H\), since \(m\psi_z^{(j)}\in\mathcal S\) and \(b(D)\) has only logarithmic growth. The dependence on z is entire in the graph norm on compact subsets of \(\mathbb C\).

The previous exact Fourier-zero selector states that, for any Xi zero z of multiplicity r, \(\psi_z^{(j)}\in(\mathbb C e_0\oplus W)^\perp\) for \(j<r\), where \(W\) is the closed span of **all** genuine theta-translation null modes. Nothing in the present proof uses RH, nor assumes these probes exhaust the complement.

## 2. Uniform high-carrier lemma for the full Gamma multiplier

**Lemma 1.** Let \(\mathcal F\) be a compact subset of Schwartz space, and let
\(F_{\Gamma,g}(x)=e^{i\Gamma x}g(x)\) with \(g\in\mathcal F\). Then
\[
\boxed{\|(b(D)-\log\Gamma)F_{\Gamma,g}\|_2\le C_{\mathcal F},
\qquad \Gamma\ge2.}\tag{4}
\]
For a bounded family of finite linear combinations of such positive and negative carriers, the estimate holds with a constant proportional to the Euclidean coefficient norm.

**Proof.** By Plancherel and the Fourier-shift identity, the square of the norm is, up to the harmless \(2\pi\) normalization,
\[
\int_{\mathbb R}|b(\pm\Gamma+u)-\log\Gamma|^2
|\widehat g(u)|^2du.
\]
On \(|u|\le\Gamma/2\), the complete symbol asymptotic (2) makes the factor \(O(1)\), uniformly for \(\Gamma\ge2\). On \(|u|>\Gamma/2\), the rough bound
\[
|b(\pm\Gamma+u)-\log\Gamma|
\le C[1+\log(2+\Gamma+|u|)]
\]
has uniformly bounded weighted \(L^2(|\widehat g|^2du)\) integral: choose Schwartz seminorm order \(N>3\), and use \(\Gamma\le2|u|\) on this outer region. The seminorm bounds are uniform on the compact \(\mathcal F\). Apply the triangle inequality for finitely many carriers. This proves (4). □

For later applications, each \(\mathcal F\) consists of finitely many functions \(m^2(x)x^j e^{\pm\eta x}\), \(j<r\), or \(m^2(x)\cosh(\eta x)\), \(m^2(x)\sinh(\eta x)/\eta\), with \(|\eta|\le1/2\). These are continuous families in Schwartz topology, including the removable value at \(\eta=0\); hence their image sets are compact.

A second elementary fact is needed. If \(\mathcal G\subset\mathcal S\) is compact, then for any \(N\),
\[
\sup_{g\in\mathcal G}\left|
\int_{\mathbb R}g(x)e^{2i\Gamma x}dx
\right|\le C_{\mathcal G,N}\Gamma^{-N}.
\tag{5}
\]
This is uniform integration by parts (or the uniform Schwartz Fourier bound). It justifies every oscillatory cross-term deletion below; **no same-frequency term is silently discarded**.

## 3. Main theorem A: an arbitrary fixed multiplicity-jet sector

Fix \(r\ge1\). Let \(\Gamma\to+\infty\), \(|\eta|\le1/2\), \(z=\Gamma+i\eta\), and
\[
v_{\mathbf a,z}=\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}a_j\psi_z^{(j)},
\quad \mathbf a\in\mathbb C^r,\qquad
P_{\mathbf a}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}a_j(ix)^j.
\tag{6}
\]
Define the exact positive Hermitian matrices (for \(k=1,2\))
\[
\mathbf G_{k,r}(\eta)[\mathbf a]
=\int_{\mathbb R}m(x)^{2k}e^{-2\eta x}
|P_{\mathbf a}(x)|^2dx.
\tag{7}
\]
They are positive definite for every \(\eta\), because a nonzero polynomial cannot vanish almost everywhere on a positive weight. Their smallest eigenvalues have strictly positive uniform lower bounds for \(|\eta|\le1/2\), by continuity and compactness.

**Theorem A.** Uniformly over \(|\eta|\le1/2\) and all coefficients \(\mathbf a\),
\[
\boxed{
\|v_{\mathbf a,z}\|_2^2
=\tfrac12\mathbf G_{1,r}(\eta)[\mathbf a]
+O_{r,N}(\Gamma^{-N})\|\mathbf a\|^2,}\tag{8}
\]
\[
\boxed{
\langle v_{\mathbf a,z},Hv_{\mathbf a,z}\rangle
=\tfrac12\log\Gamma\,
\mathbf G_{2,r}(\eta)[\mathbf a]
+O_r(\|\mathbf a\|^2).}\tag{9}
\]
In particular, writing
\[
\mu_r=\min_{|\eta|\le1/2}\ \min_{\mathbf a\ne0}
\frac{\mathbf G_{2,r}(\eta)[\mathbf a]}
{\mathbf G_{1,r}(\eta)[\mathbf a]}>0,
\]
there is a finite \(\Gamma_r\) such that, for every \(\Gamma\ge\Gamma_r\),
\[
\boxed{\langle v,Hv\rangle\ge
\tfrac12\mu_r\log\Gamma\,\|v\|_2^2
\quad\text{for all }v\in
\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}
\{\psi_{\Gamma+i\eta}^{(j)}:0\le j<r\}.}\tag{10}
\]
The threshold is independent of \(\eta\). Thus every fixed-size multiplicity-jet subspace belonging to an actual Xi zero of sufficiently high real ordinate has strictly **positive** original H-form.

**Proof.** The exact exponential decomposition is
\[
v_{\mathbf a,z}(x)=\frac{m(x)}2\left[
e^{i\Gamma x-\eta x}P_{\mathbf a}(x)
+e^{-i\Gamma x+\eta x}P_{\mathbf a}(-x)\right].
\tag{11}
\]
Apply (5) to its two-carrier squared norm. The two diagonal integrals are equal by reflection \(x\mapsto-x\), and each is exactly (7) for \(k=1\). Their total coefficient is \(1/2\). The cross integral is \(O_{r,N}(\Gamma^{-N})\|\mathbf a\|^2\), uniformly in \(\eta\). This proves (8). The identical calculation for \(mv\) gives
\[
\|mv\|_2^2=\tfrac12\mathbf G_{2,r}(\eta)[\mathbf a]
+O_{r,N}(\Gamma^{-N})\|\mathbf a\|^2.
\tag{12}
\]
The source \(mv\) is a finite sum of two high carriers with uniformly bounded Schwartz envelopes. Lemma 1 yields
\[
\langle mv,b(D)mv\rangle
=\log\Gamma\,\|mv\|_2^2+O_r(\|\mathbf a\|^2).
\]
Here \(\|mv\|_2=O_r(\|\mathbf a\|)\) uniformly, so Cauchy-Schwarz justifies the error in the *quadratic form*, and \((\log\Gamma)\Gamma^{-N}=O(1)\). The **whole original signed prime potential** contributes
\(|\langle v,Vv\rangle|\le\|V\|\|v\|^2=O_r(\|\mathbf a\|^2)\).
Subtracting yields (9). Uniform definiteness of both (7) matrices converts (8)–(9) into
\[
\frac{H[v]}{\|v\|^2}
=\log\Gamma\,
\frac{\mathbf G_{2,r}(\eta)[\mathbf a]}
{\mathbf G_{1,r}(\eta)[\mathbf a]}
+O_r(1)
\]
uniformly for nonzero \(\mathbf a\), and choosing a sufficiently high \(\Gamma_r\) gives (10). □

This does not require a lower bound for a **global** unknown complement. The subspace dimension r is fixed *before* the high-ordinate limit; no threshold uniform in r is asserted.

## 4. Main theorem B: the entire conjugate two-plane, uniformly through \(\eta=0\)

Theorem A for r=1 treats one complex line. For a hypothetical off-critical zero, \(\Xi(z)=\Xi(\bar z)=0\), so its two complex-conjugate probe vectors span a potentially more dangerous real two-plane. Positivity of the individual diagonal Rayleigh quotients would not imply positivity of this plane. We prove its **full mixed form**, including the near-coalescence regime.

For \(0\le\eta\le1/2\) define
\[
A_\eta(x)=\cosh(\eta x),\qquad
B_\eta(x)=\begin{cases}\sinh(\eta x)/\eta,&\eta>0,\\ x,&\eta=0,\end{cases}
\tag{13}
\]
and the real even probe vectors
\[
U_{\Gamma,\eta}=m(x)A_\eta(x)\cos(\Gamma x),
\quad
T_{\Gamma,\eta}=m(x)B_\eta(x)\sin(\Gamma x).
\tag{14}
\]
The second vector is **confluently normalized** by \(1/\eta\), avoiding a false conditioning singularity at \(\eta=0\). For \(z=\Gamma+i\eta\),
\[
\boxed{\psi_z=U_{\Gamma,\eta}-i\eta T_{\Gamma,\eta},
\quad\psi_{\bar z}=U_{\Gamma,\eta}+i\eta T_{\Gamma,\eta}.}\tag{15}
\]
For \(k=1,2\) put
\[
J_{k,A}(\eta)=\int m^{2k}A_\eta^2dx,\qquad
J_{k,B}(\eta)=\int m^{2k}B_\eta^2dx.
\tag{16}
\]
All four are finite, continuous on the compact interval, and strictly positive; \(J_{k,B}(0)=\int x^2m^{2k}dx>0\).

**Theorem B.** Uniformly for \(0\le\eta\le1/2\), \(\Gamma\to+\infty\), and \(a,b\in\mathbb C\), put \(v=aU_{\Gamma,\eta}+bT_{\Gamma,\eta}\). Then
\[
\boxed{\|v\|^2=
\tfrac12(|a|^2J_{1,A}+|b|^2J_{1,B})
+O_N(\Gamma^{-N})(|a|^2+|b|^2),}\tag{17}
\]
\[
\boxed{H[v]=\tfrac12\log\Gamma
(|a|^2J_{2,A}+|b|^2J_{2,B})
+O(|a|^2+|b|^2).}\tag{18}
\]
Set
\[
\mu_{\rm pair}:=
\min_{0\le\eta\le1/2}
\min\left\{\frac{J_{2,A}(\eta)}{J_{1,A}(\eta)},
\frac{J_{2,B}(\eta)}{J_{1,B}(\eta)}\right\}>0.
\tag{19}
\]
Then for some finite \(\Gamma_{\rm pair}\), **independent of the distance to the line**,
\[
\boxed{H[v]\ge\tfrac12\mu_{\rm pair}\log\Gamma\,\|v\|^2
\qquad (\Gamma\ge\Gamma_{\rm pair},\
v\in\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}\{U_{\Gamma,\eta},T_{\Gamma,\eta}\}).}\tag{20}
\]

**Proof.** The weighted cosine-square, sine-square, and cosine-sine products reduce to integrals of \(m^{2k}A_\eta^2\cos(2\Gamma x)\), \(m^{2k}B_\eta^2\cos(2\Gamma x)\), and \(m^{2k}A_\eta B_\eta\sin(2\Gamma x)\). The nonoscillatory terms are **diagonal** because \(\cos\Gamma x\sin\Gamma x\) has zero mean. All oscillatory integrals are \(O_N(\Gamma^{-N})\), uniformly on compact \(\eta\), by (5) and Schwartz bounds. This gives (17) and its \(m v\) analogue with \(k=2\). Lemma 1 and the bounded complete potential \(V\) then prove (18), exactly as in Theorem A. Uniform positive minimum of the two ratios in (19) and the positive physical Gram give (20). □

If \(\Xi(\Gamma+i\eta)=0\) with \(\eta\ne0\), then \(\Xi(\Gamma-i\eta)=0\), and the **entire** complex plane spanned by those two original complementary probes is precisely the plane in (20), hence has positive H-form for sufficiently high ordinate. This is unconditional as a statement about **any hypothetical** off-line zero; it does not assert such a zero exists. At \(\eta=0\), the limiting second vector \(mx\sin\Gamma x\) need not be in the zero-probe complement for a simple zero; the analytic two-plane inequality itself remains valid.

## 5. Explicit one-vector asymptotic and its exact scope

For a single arbitrary strip point \(z=\Gamma+i\eta\), Theorem A with r=1 yields
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\langle\psi_z,H\psi_z\rangle}{\|\psi_z\|^2}
=\log\Gamma\,
\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb R}m^4(x)\cosh(2\eta x)dx}
{\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb R}m^2(x)\cosh(2\eta x)dx}
+O(1),}\tag{21}
\]
uniformly for \(|\eta|\le1/2\). To see the \(\cosh(2\eta x)\) form directly, use
\[
|\cos((\Gamma+i\eta)x)|^2
=\tfrac12[\cosh(2\eta x)+\cos(2\Gamma x)]
\]
and the parity of \(m\). The coefficient in (21) is **strictly positive**, uniformly over the entire critical strip. The numerical values in the optional sanity program are not used to establish positivity.

## 6. Consequence for full RH and identified failure mechanism

Previous work proved that any hypothetical off-line zero yields nonzero original-domain vectors orthogonal to the entire known theta-translation kernel, but **did not determine the sign** of H on them. The present full-operator theorem determines a substantial part of that sign:

* A single zero-indexed probe is high-ordinate positive for every possible real part of the zero, including off-line.
* Every fixed-multiplicity jet span belonging to one zero is high-ordinate positive, with a uniform threshold over the full strip.
* Even the full two-plane of conjugate probes from an off-line quartet has high-ordinate positive form, **uniformly as the quartet approaches the critical line**. The normalization by \(\eta\) prevents an artificial near-collision negative-sign inference.

Thus **an argument that attempts to eliminate all off-line zeros by finding a negative H Rayleigh quotient in each individual zero-probe or conjugate-probe plane cannot succeed at large heights**. Its needed obstruction must involve genuinely global combinations, different test vectors, or an additional mechanism. Conversely this theorem cannot be used to deduce H>=0 on all of \(e_0^\perp\): positivity on the union of small-dimensional moving subspaces does not imply positivity on their closed span or its orthogonal complement. The original Weil sign / RH step remains open.
