**Scope.** Analytic audit of Part I of [ACTUAL_NEGATIVE_BAND_COVERAGE.md](../20261012_adaptive_spectral_tail/ACTUAL_NEGATIVE_BAND_COVERAGE.md) at repository HEAD `3c1f2b8`. This audit covers weighted Fourier moments of the paired prime coefficients, bounded regularized multipliers, the single and double commutators, preservation of the original operator domain, the whole-band Lyapunov estimate, the limit as `h -> 0`, and the small-depth exponents. It does not audit the numerical gap or any critical positivity claim.

**Disposition: PASS for the stated positive-depth regularity result, conditional on the cited original theta-strip envelope and original operator/domain foundations.** I found no algebraic sign, domain, low-frequency circularity, or prime-summability flaw in Part I. The result is a whole-band upper bound and supplies no sign of the original signed operator at critical depth. The strip envelope for the actual `m` is an input from `ACTUAL_FULL_GAMMA_CERTIFICATE.md`, Appendix B; its analytic derivation is covered by the separate [theta-strip and eigenstate-tail audit](THETA_STRIP_AND_TAILS.md).

Here a strip-envelope L1 bound means a uniform bound for \(\int_{\mathbb R}|a(x+iy)|\,dx\) over the cited strip; it is a bound used in the Cauchy-circle argument, not a new function space.

## Checks

1. **Weighted moments and the complete prime series.** With `a_p(a)=(2pi)^(-1) int <t>^p |ahat(t)| dt`, Cauchy circles of radius `1/4` bound the `k`th derivative's L1 norm by `k!4^k` times the strip L1 norm. Splitting at `|t|=1`, and taking `k=ceil(p)+2`, gives `a_p(a) <= B_p ||a||_{L1(strip-envelope)}` with the displayed `B_p=2^(p/2)(1+k!4^k)`; the normalization `dt/(2pi)` is consistent. The bounds `||m||_1<40/3`, `||m^2||_1<25`, and
   `||m(.)m(.+log n)||_1 <= 100 n^2(log n+1)e^(-3n/2)` follow from the stated envelope by splitting `x` into `[-log n,0]` and its two exterior rays. Since `alpha_n=Lambda(n)/sqrt(n) <= log n/sqrt(n)`, the stated coarse majorant `2 sum alpha_n S_n <=400 sum_{n>=2} n^3 e^(-3n/2) <400(149/108)` is valid (use `log n<=sqrt n` and `log n+1<=2sqrt n`). Thus the complete absolute weighted coefficient sum is bounded by `P_p=800 B_p` for every fixed `p`; `n` ranges over every integer with `Lambda(n) != 0`, hence all prime powers are included. The translated terms are kept as paired coefficients `m(x)m(x +/- log n)`: no divergent bare sum `sum alpha_n` or arbitrary unweighted `S` is used. Separately, the lower-form estimate pays the omitted full prime tail through the cited original operator-norm bound `delta_N`; this bound also sums over every omitted prime power.

2. **Regularized multiplier and the single commutator.** For `x=<t>`, `l_(s,h)=(x/(1+hx))^s` has derivative `s x^(s-1) x'/(1+hx)^(s+1)`, so uniformly in `h`, `|l'(t)|<=s<t>^(s-1)`. The two-sided Peetre inequality yields (6), including `1/2<=s<1` where the exponent `s-1` is negative. With `q=s-1/2`, the Gamma symbol's `b(u)<=7<u>^(1/2)` leaves exactly the `H^q` input weight in the two kernels for `[L,B]`; the stated `p0,p1` coefficient moments and multiplication bound for `m:H^q->H^q` give the constants in (4). For `V`, `L` commutes with each translate, so only the difference against its paired coefficient appears, and the `P_p0` moment controls the absolutely convergent full sum. The displayed bound is uniform in `h` as an `H^q -> L2` bound.

3. **Actual operator domain.** At each fixed `h>0`, `L_(s,h)` maps Schwartz functions to Schwartz functions. Its derivative also satisfies `|l'(t)|<=s h^(-s-1)<t>^(-2)`, giving the manuscript's fixed-`h` difference bound `2s h^(-s-1)|t-u|<t-u>^2<u>^(-2)`. This makes the fixed-`h` commutator bounded on `L2`, with the logarithmic `b` absorbed by the decaying frequency weight. The multiplier maps the cited even `C_c^infinity` operator graph core for `B` into the original domain. Since `V` is bounded, this is also a graph core for `H`; closedness then gives `L D(H) subset D(H)` and the identity (7) on all of `D(H)`. In particular, the proof does not apply `b(D)` to an unqualified form-domain source. In the double-commutator argument, `Q` has bounded spectral support, `LQ` maps into `D(H)` at fixed `h`, and the bounded fixed-`h` commutators justify the operator algebra before the uniform quadratic-form estimate is invoked.

4. **Low-frequency estimate is noncircular.** For `|t|<=T`, Peetre gives `<u>^s <=2^(s/2)<T>^s<t-u>^s`; also `l_(s,h)(u)<=<u>^s`. Weighted Young convolution therefore proves `||P_T m L_(s,h)||_(2->2)<=2^(s/2)<T>^s A_s` on the entire original `L2` space. This estimate has no `Q`-regularity premise and applies to `LQv` before any Sobolev conclusion. `Qv` is in `D(H)` because its spectrum is in the bounded interval `[-1/2,-kappa]`; the fixed-`h` domain preservation supplies the legal form-domain input to (9).

5. **Double-commutator sign, weights, and Lyapunov inverse.** Under `[A,B]=AB-BA`, the Fourier kernel of `[L,[L,H]]` has multiplier `(l(t)-l(u))^2`; exactly,
   `2 L H L = L^2 H + H L^2 - [L,[L,H]]`.
   With `W=LQ`, `X=W*W=QL^2Q`, and `H|Ran Q=-K`, this yields
   `2W*HW=-XK-KX-Q[L,[L,H]]Q`, with the sign shown in the manuscript. For the translated potential, squaring (6), dividing the kernel by `<t>^q<u>^q`, and applying Peetre leaves coefficient weight `<t-u>^p<u>^-beta`; the input factor is `<=1`. For `B`, writing `k1=t-z`, `k2=z-u`, the symbol bound `b(z)<=G_beta 2^(beta/2)<u>^beta<k2>^beta` cancels the residual input power exactly: `2s-2+beta-2q=0`. The remaining `p` moment and two normalized Young inequalities give the displayed `D_(s,rho)`; this is a quadratic-form bound on `H^q`, not an unclaimed uniform `L2` operator norm.

   From (9), the noncircular estimate above, and `delta_N<kappa`, one obtains
   `(K-delta_N)X+X(K-delta_N) <= [2 c_N L_s(T)^2 + D_(s,rho)M_q^2] I`.
   On `Ran Q`, `A=K-delta_N >= a I`, `a=kappa-delta_N>=kappa/2`. The order-preserving inverse of `X -> AX+XA` is `Y -> int_0^infinity e^(-tA)Y e^(-tA)dt`; applied to `I`, it is `(2A)^(-1)` and has norm at most `1/(2a)`. Thus `||X|| <= [c_N L_s(T)^2+(D_(s,rho)/2)M_q^2]/a`, exactly yielding (13). These orders and factors are consistent.

6. **`h -> 0` and asymptotic cost.** For each `v`, `l_(s,h)^2` increases pointwise to `<t>^(2s)` as `h` decreases to zero. Monotone convergence converts the uniform-in-`h` bound to the `H^s` operator bound at grid orders; Fourier Holder interpolation gives (14) for all real `r>=0`. Choosing `N` from `delta_N<=kappa/2` gives `N=O(log(1/kappa))`, `c_N=O(sqrt(L)log L)` with `L=log(1/kappa)`, and `T=exp(c_N)`. Hence `L_s(T)` costs only `kappa^(-o(1))` for fixed `s`. The depth-dependent `rho=1-1/(2+sqrt L)` has `beta=2(1-rho)=O(L^(-1/2))`, so `G_beta=O(sqrt L)`; for fixed target order only a bounded number of recursion steps occur, and all moment indices and factorial constants remain bounded. Iterating the recurrence gives exponent `r/(2rho)=r/2+o(1)`, with the stated other constants subpower. This establishes only strictly positive-depth bounds.
