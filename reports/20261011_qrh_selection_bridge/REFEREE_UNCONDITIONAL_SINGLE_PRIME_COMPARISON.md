## Part A. Uniform large auxiliary modulus

### 1. Actual common-class expansion and uniformity

The Kummer coordinate [m/c^2] is independent of n. Thus Minkowski in the actual n column can be applied before the pair expansion, ensuring the same n on both sides. With c=ga and c'=ga', the exact zero masks give h=g^4 a^4(a')^4 h0 and m=(a')^4 h0 z^6, m'=a^4 h0(z')^6. The finite original gamma sectors and units remain retained.

The residual moving character is exactly chi_a bar(chi_a') bar(chi_s) chi_s'. Its conductor contains no large auxiliary prime. Its good-prime exponent lies between -2 and 2, so its principal condition is precisely a s'=a' s, or s=av, s'=a'v. No false condition (v,g)=1 is inserted. Fixed bad-ray expansions can only remove some of these principal alternatives and cost a fixed factor.

The sixth-power-free Dirichlet series L(s,theta)/L(6s,theta^6), with all canceled conductor zeros restored as holes, is exact. On Re s=3/4 the denominator is absolutely controlled. Large b holes are paid by exp(C sum_(p|b) P^-3/4), using precisely the hypothesis of the theorem. Small holes cost (NM)^epsilon. Shifting the smooth Mellin transform therefore gives the principal residue plus H^(3/4) times a fixed polynomial conductor bound, with no B^epsilon or log B. The residual ray character is finite order over the fixed imaginary quadratic field. The subunit H case is harmless since its artificial residue is O(H)<=O(H^3/4); it is not counted as a nonempty literal support.

## Part B. Complete single-prime high comparison

### 1. The prime theorem really applies

I independently opened and read [Kai, arXiv:2209.11816v3, Theorem 1.2.1 and section 2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2209.11816v3#S1.SS2). The fixed-field theorem permits moduli bounded by each fixed logarithmic power of the prime scale. In this class-number-one imaginary quadratic field, radial smoothing and summing residue-class estimates against a nonprincipal finite-order character yield the displayed ideal-character mean. The polynomial number of residue classes is absorbed into the square-root-log exponential error. A norm scale P corresponds to an archimedean radius comparable to sqrt(P), which changes only constants and a harmless fixed margin in the logarithmic exponent. Possible real exceptional characters are included: the logarithmic-power range allows their contribution to be absorbed with ineffective constants. No effective modulus-uniform zero-free assertion is being assumed.

For P=exp(Z), every fixed raw box Nu<=Z^L has true conductor at most 6^12 Z^(5L), below (log P)^(5L+2) for sufficiently large Z. The constant L is chosen once for a desired error power, while the physical prime law P=exp(Z) remains unchanged. This is fundamentally different from applying the same theorem to a prime pool only polylogarithmic in Z.

The conductor bound retains the bad primes. For the Kummer extension generated by a sixth root of the actual element u, the minimal polynomial is an integral factor of X^6-u. The polynomial discriminant factorization, relative field discriminant divisibility, and conductor-discriminant identity give the stated upper bound. Since K contains the sixth roots of unity, the relevant character is faithful on this abelian extension. A sixth-power-free element is a sixth power in K only when it is the unit 1. Thus all five nontrivial unit representatives are nonprincipal as well. No conditioning on a ray where a character might become principal is performed.

### 4. Consequence and exact open estimate

The independently reviewed uniform-B positive energy theorem applies to the same one-prime law with B comparable to exp(Z). Its first norm term is Z^epsilon Y^-1/2 and its error B^-1/8 Z^C is exponentially small. Squaring gives the announced positive energy exponent -23/48. This is not borrowed from the earlier polynomial-B proof.

A one-sided bound for the actual original-Mobius signed off-class sum by Z^-3/8+epsilon would then imply the whole averaged low estimate by Jensen and the fixed finite ray-sector Cauchy inequality. The two-stage principal-abscissa argument is genuinely noncircular: begin on a fixed line above one, obtain a conditional fixed zero-free strip from that hypothetical low bound, and only then use the supremum of real parts of zeros. A positive contour margin can be frozen before approaching the supremum. This proves a sufficient implication, not the missing bound and not a necessity theorem for the stronger sector-expanded target.

The present synthesis genuinely removes the separate prime-mean hypothesis for this enlarged observation. It does not establish cancellation of the original long-Mobius cross correlations. Consequently no new zeta zero has been excluded and no RH conclusion is obtained.
