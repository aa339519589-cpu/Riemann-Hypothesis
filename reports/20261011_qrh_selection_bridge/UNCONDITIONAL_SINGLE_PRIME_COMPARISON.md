## Part A. Uniform large-modulus positive Kummer energy

### 1. The physical object and theorem

Use the positive admissible orientation xi=xi0 chi_b, eta=1, original Gaussian and radial profiles, half n damping, either original/core nonflat marker, and the actual finite compensation. Let b be squarefree outside the original bad data and the slot supports, B=N(b0 b). Do NOT assume B is polynomial in Z. Instead suppose

             sum_(p|b) (Np)^(-3/4) <= C_aux.                    (1)

In particular any single arbitrarily large good prime satisfies (1). The constants below may depend on C_aux and the original fixed data, but not on B. Set X=Z^(17/48), Y=Z^(23/48), ell=1/6, and keep 0<T<=Z. A compensation term has actual coefficient (ND)^(-3/2), scales X'=X/ND, Y'=Y/ND, T'=T NE, with E the marked tuple. This bound has no factor B^epsilon. In the range B>=exp(Z^a), for any fixed a>0, (2) gives the same Z^(-23/96+epsilon) positive-block bound. The signed off-class sum is not estimated by (2).

### 2. A sixth-power-free character mean with a separated large hole

Fix the number field K=Q(omega) and its original primary conventions. Finitely many unit and fixed bad-ray choices may be separated, at fixed cost. Let theta be a finite-order Hecke character with primitive conductor q, with all further zero masks kept as holes. Let h run over sixth-power-free ideals in one of these fixed choices. The unfrozen fixed-ray restriction can be expanded into finitely many fixed characters; the principal alternatives in that expansion have the same good-prime condition below.

If V is a compact smooth norm test in a fixed annulus and H>0, the exact Dirichlet series is

 sum_(h sixth-power-free,(h,Mb)=1) theta(h)(Nh)^(-s)
   = L(s,theta)/L(6s,theta^6)
     *product_(p|Mb) (1-theta(p)P^(-s))/(1-theta(p)^6 P^(-6s)). (3)

Here primitive zero values make redundant factors equal to one. The finite factors at canceled conductor primes are retained by M. On Re s=3/4 the denominator in (3) is absolutely controlled. The product over b is bounded by exp(C sum_(p|b)P^(-3/4)), uniformly in the entire height variable. It is NOT bounded by B^epsilon. The product over the remaining M is <<_epsilon (NM)^epsilon. The ordinary primitive functional equation and convexity give a polynomial in Nq and height, with fixed degree and no auxiliary modulus in q.

Mellin inversion shifted from a line greater than one to Re s=3/4 therefore gives

 sum_h theta(h) V(Nh/H) 1_((h,Mb)=1)
   = 1_(theta principal) rho_(M,b) H integral_0^infinity V(t)dt
     +O_V(H^(3/4)(Nq)^C (NM)^epsilon),                      (4)

where 0<=rho_(M,b)<=C_K. The residue is at most the fixed unrestricted ideal density; deleted local factors are (1-P^-1)/(1-P^-6)<=1. In a finite ray expansion, the same upper bound holds up to its fixed cost. Formula (4) is uniform even for H<1; both the absent actual sum and the main term are dominated by the error there. No Q is used.

The parameter M in the application below is made only from c,c',n,s,s' and fixed data. It has no prime factor from b. No derivative of the b Euler product is required in this Mellin proof, so a power of log B is not introduced.

### 3. Actual pair parametrization: the auxiliary character cancels

First take physical s squarefree. Apply Minkowski in n BEFORE the pair expansion, so n is the SAME fixed ideal in the two copies. This is permitted by its original outside weight (Nn)^(-3/2). Its marked divisibility is retained later.

For a fixed common h, write

 c=g a, c'=g a', (g,a)=(g,a')=(a,a')=1,
 h=g^4 a^4 (a')^4 h0,
 m=(a')^4 h0 z^6,     m'=a^4 h0 (z')^6.                  (5)

All of g,a,a' are squarefree, h0 is sixth-power-free, and (h0,g a a')=1. Other primes of valuation four may occur in h0; they are NOT excluded. Formula (5) follows from the exact (c,m)=(c',m')=1 support and retains the finite unit choices. The original m unit conditions impose further row/z restrictions and the common h0 holes; none is replaced by a larger character value on a nonunit.

The original xi(m) and conjugate(xi(m')) factors have the SAME xi(h0), which cancels exactly because xi has order dividing six. The full auxiliary root has modulus one and cancels in the pair norm. All residual xi, Gauss, reciprocity, and unit factors are bounded coefficients independent of h0. This is a cancellation in the actual identity, not an estimate using the conductor of xi.

The only moving good character of h0 is

             theta = chi_a conjugate(chi_a')
                     conjugate(chi_s) chi_s'.             (6)

The common n factor chi_n^3 cancels. Canceled-prime zero masks remain holes, as in (3). Its primitive conductor is at most a fixed multiple of rad(a a' s s'); in particular it does not contain b. The finite gamma restrictions only restrict the original s,s' coefficients.

Since all good local characters have order six and the integer local exponent in (6) lies between -2 and 2, the principal good-character condition is exactly

       a s'=a' s,   hence   s=a v,  s'=a' v,               (7)

where v is squarefree and prime to aa'. There is no new condition (v,g)=1. Fixed bad factors can further restrict (7); ignoring that restriction only enlarges the positive main-term majorant.

The two actual norm windows give h0 scales

 H1= B X' Ns / [(Na')^4 (Nz)^6],
 H2= B X' Ns'/ [(Na)^4 (Nz')^6].                          (8)

If the product of radial profiles is nonzero, H1/H2 lies in a fixed compact interval. Apply (4) with H=sqrt(H1 H2); all its profile seminorms are uniformly bounded. This remains true without separating the moving s-window into Mellin modes. If the actual h0 support is nonempty, both H_i are bounded below by a fixed positive constant; otherwise the actual sum is zero and the uniform form of (4) still bounds its residue/error decomposition.

On the principal alternative (7), the residue bound in (4), divided by the EXACT radial normalization B X' sqrt(Ns Ns'), is

      << (Na Na')^(-2) (Nz Nz')^(-3).                     (9)

This is the actual lcm(c,c')^4 density. The large B cancels here, rather than being paid by a pointwise divisor-count bound for h. Summing z,z' in (9) is absolutely convergent. Retaining their zero masks only decreases this positive majorant.

### 4. The unmarked principal energy, uniform for every Gaussian scale

The outside pair c factor is (Ng)^(-1)(Na Na')^(-1/2). Because Ns,Ns' lie in the fixed Y' annulus, (7) forces a,a' to have comparable norm and permits at most O(Y'/max(Na,Na')) choices of v. Thus, for each fixed n, the principal energy is bounded by

 C/Y' *sum_(g,a,a') (Ng)^(-1)(Na Na')^(-5/2)
                    /max(Na,Na')
 * V_G(Ng Na (Nn)^3/T') V_G(Ng Na' (Nn)^3/T').          (10)

Use the original logarithmic Gaussian, not a hard c cutoff. Its harmonic norm satisfies

       sup_(U>0) sum_g (Ng)^(-1) |V_G(Ng/U)|^2 < infinity. (11)

Indeed each norm dyad has O(1) harmonic ideal mass and the logarithmic Gaussian is summable uniformly in the translated log scale; for U<1 its positive-norm tail only improves the bound. Cauchy in g applies (11) to the two distinct scales in (10). The remaining a,a' sum is absolutely convergent, even if the comparability and coprimality restrictions are removed. This proves the uniform fixed-n principal norm O((Y')^(-1/2)). Summing the actual n^(-3/2) weight by Minkowski converges.

### 5. The marked-c tuple is paid by an lcm kernel, not by a long h divisor bound

Fix the rescaled tuple D and the disjoint assignment of marked slots to c or n, using the original priority p|c. Freeze the n-marked tuple En; the same n is a multiple of En and every remaining mask is retained. Let Ec and Ec' be the possibly different actual tuples of c-marked primes in the two pair copies. Their Gaussian scales are T NEc NEn and T NEc' NEn, respectively.

In (5), split these tuples uniquely as

 Ec=Eg Ea, Eg|g, Ea|a;
 Ec'=Eg' Ea', Eg'|g, Ea'|a'.

A prime occurring on both sides can occur in these tuples simultaneously only in Eg and Eg', since g,a,a' are pairwise coprime. Set G0=lcm(Eg,Eg'). The g harmonic sum in (10), with g a multiple of G0, is at most C/NG0. To see the actual Gaussian normalization, substitute g=G0 g0, a=Ea a0, a'=Ea' a0'. The factors Ea and Ea' cancel their respective marked Gaussian scales. The two remaining harmonic scales are

 T NEn NEg / [NG0 Na0 (Nn)^3],
 T NEn NEg'/ [NG0 Na0' (Nn)^3].

Both are legitimate arbitrary positive scales for (11), so there is no ratio or power of B or of the marked primes hidden in this step.

The forced a,a' sums in (10) cost at most (NEa NEa')^(-5/2). Hence the pair weight for Ec,Ec' is bounded by

                C/[N lcm(Eg,Eg') (NEa NEa')^(5/2)].       (12)

For a single fixed slot pool, its g/g contribution is

 sum_(p,p') W_p W_p'/N lcm(p,p')
 <= sum_p W_p^2/P + (sum_p W_p/P)^2 <<1.                 (13)

The g/a and a/a contributions are smaller, using sum W/P^(5/2)<<1. There are only finitely many assignments of the fixed slot set. Equation (13) prices every marked-c pool uniformly, including its diagonal, without a factor (Nh)^epsilon.

For En, Minkowski in the actual n-column gives sum_(En|n)(Nn)^(-3/2)<< (NEn)^(-3/2), uniformly in the Gaussian scale by (11). Its marked-prime l1 sums are bounded. Rescaled slots have the exact factor

       (ND)^(-3/2)(Y/ND)^(-1/2)=Y^(-1/2)(ND)^(-1),

with bounded outer sums. This proves the marked principal-energy norm O(Y^(-1/2)) before the harmless fixed slot normalizer. Ordinary/core masks only restrict these positive majorants; the original priority and the same class label are retained.

### 6. The complete nonprincipal error contains a NEGATIVE power of B

For a general s,s' pair, the error in (4), divided by the true radial normalization, is

 << (B X' sqrt(Ns Ns'))^(-1/4)
       (Na Na')^(-3/2)(Nz Nz')^(-9/4)

Here q divides fixed data times a a' s s'; M uses only c,c',n,s,s' and fixed data. All z,z' sums in (14) converge absolutely. There is no auxiliary conductor in q, and (1) already paid its sole remaining hole factor.

 |V_G(c n^3/T')| <= C_N (T'/(Nc (Nn)^3))^N.

This dominates every polynomial c-conductor/hole factor, gives a convergent c sum, and leaves summable negative n powers. The s support is finite of scale Y', and all finitely many slot sums cost at most a fixed power of Z. Thus, after Minkowski in n and all compensation terms, the error in the squared positive norm is at most

                    B^(-1/4) Z^C,                       (15)

with some fixed C independent of B and all T<=Z. Taking its square root gives B^(-1/8) Z^C after enlarging C. The principal part was estimated by its positive majorant in sections 4--5; no termwise sign of the true pair phases is assumed. The signed remainder may always be dominated by (15).

### 3. A genuine finite-order character theorem in its allowed range

For fixed K and fixed A>0, the fixed-field Siegel--Walfisz theorem gives, for every nonprincipal finite-order Hecke character theta with true primitive conductor norm q<=(log P)^A,

 |sum_(l good) W(Nl/P)theta(l)| / sum_(l good)W(Nl/P)
       <<_(K,A,W) exp(-c_(K,A) sqrt(log P)).              (6)

The constants may be ineffective, but are uniform in the character and q in this range. A primary source is Wataru Kai, Notes on Mitsui's Prime Number Theorem with Siegel zeros, arXiv:2209.11816v3 (8 September 2026), Theorem 1.2.1, together with section 2.3:
 https://arxiv.org/html/2209.11816v3#S1.SS2
 https://arxiv.org/html/2209.11816v3#S2.SS3 .

To obtain the displayed ideal formulation from Theorem 1.2.1, apply its fixed-ray prime-element estimate to radial disks/sectors, take their finite differences and fixed smooth partial summation, then sum the ray-class estimates against theta. Its principal term vanishes. The at-most-polynomial-in-q number of class errors is a fixed power of log P, absorbed by decreasing c_(K,A); the primary-generator/unit choices are finite. This is the all-good prime sum, not a ray on which theta could restrict to the principal character.

The possible real exceptional character has not been discarded. In the equivalent form of Theorem 2.3.2 it contributes -P^beta/beta. In the logarithmic-power modulus range the fixed-field Siegel bound gives 1-beta >= c_epsilon q^(-epsilon). For q<=(log P)^A choose any fixed epsilon<1/(2A). It follows that P^(beta-1) is at most exp(-c sqrt(log P)) for sufficiently large P, with an ineffective constant. This is exactly the standard source of ineffectivity in (6), not a new zero-free hypothesis. Characters of the other finite orders have no real exceptional case.

### 4. Every raw row in the truncation has the required conductor

Fix an arbitrary desired power N>0. Choose a fixed L>N+8, and in the actual five ideal sums restrict

         Nc,Nn,Ns,Na,Nu <= Z^L.                          (7)

All omitted terms have total absolute mass O(Z^(-L)): the slowest of the five tails in (4) are the s and u sums of exponent two. The statement keeps the full ideal variables; it is not a special compact subset substituted for the actual probe.

For u!=1 in (7), theta_u(l)=chi_l(u) is a NONPRINCIPAL finite-order Kummer Hecke character over the fixed field K, of order dividing six. This includes the five nontrivial unit classes. The exact good-plus-bad conductor estimate is

                N f_(theta_u) <= 6^12 (Nu)^5.            (8)

Indeed K contains the sixth roots of unity. The nontrivial Kummer extension K(u^(1/6))/K is abelian of degree dividing six. The polynomial X^6-u has discriminant 6^6 u^5; if it is reducible, use its monic integral minimal factor and the complementary resultant identity. The relative field discriminant divides the resulting polynomial discriminant. The conductor-discriminant formula then bounds each constituent character conductor by its norm, which is (8).
