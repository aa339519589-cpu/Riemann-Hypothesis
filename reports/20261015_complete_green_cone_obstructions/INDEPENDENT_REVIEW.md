# Mathematical review and dependency scope

Date: 2026-10-15.

The two constructions were checked independently against the complete
positive-form kernels before preparation of this report. The publication
review checks the written proofs, not finite numerical diagnostics.
These are internal mathematical reviews, not formal verification or
external referee endorsements.

The unchanged forms, quotient, Riesz inverse, and bounded gradient map
come from the published full-prime Green construction. The published
reflected-energy report supplies the source's simple zero, positive
compact bounds, and complete theta tails. Chebyshev's bound is used for
complete incoming rows; Bertrand's theorem selects one genuine prime
for the first lower bound. No conjectural zero information is used.

The derivative proof retains the complete Gamma, pole, forward and
reflected kernels. Its uniform P-column lower bound is split into three
ranges; its N-column separation uses the integer gap above a prime and
one Bertrand prime below twice that prime. Smoothing makes both loads
ordinary compact functions. The order contradiction uses the complete
variational pairing, not an inverse on delta columns or a finite
Galerkin inverse.

For the oriented-edge proof, endpoint ratios are pointwise transported
psi ratios. This makes both loads annihilate psi exactly. Annihilating
psi as a dual load is not the same as choosing the pole-centered test
section: the latter is used only for the complete remainder estimate.
The past-prime image is paid in the inverse pole weight, not asserted to
belong to ordinary L2. The logarithmic Gamma scaling gives a bounded
full form-dual residual, and the additional dual-norm estimate supplies
the O(log(1/epsilon)^(-3/2)) inverse-cross error.

The edge theorem is on the ambient oriented-edge cone. Its positive
packets need not be gradients, and gradient projection need not preserve
positivity. The three-reflection sign product only excludes independent
edge-sign gauges of that cone. The complete transfer-current operator
remains Hilbert positive semidefinite throughout.

The conclusions do not decide C<=I, do not exclude all cones or eventual
positivity, and do not give an upper bound for the actual arithmetic
load. No RH conclusion is asserted.
