## Round 189 — A/B result: the approximated-\(\xi\) limit argument does not produce RH progress

**Source inspected:** Enrico P. G. Cadeddu, *Proof of the Riemann Hypothesis
through the limit of an approximated \(\xi\) function*, latest Zenodo version
examined, record 21243275:
<https://zenodo.org/records/21243275>.

The decisive calculation is independent of any numerical experiment.  Let
\[
\Lambda(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s),
\]
or include the nonzero polynomial factor to obtain the usual completed
\(\xi\)-function.  The functional equation gives
\[
\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)
\tag{189.1}
\]
as an identity of meromorphic/entire functions.  Hence, for every path
\(s(Q)\to s_0\) on which the denominator is nonzero,
\[
\frac{\xi(s(Q))}{\xi(1-s(Q))}=1
\tag{189.2}
\]
identically, regardless of \(\operatorname{Re}s_0\).  If \(s_0\) is a zero,
one simply chooses points of the path away from the discrete zero set; the
local ratio is still identically one.  At the zero itself the quotient is
undefined, not a source of a contradiction.

The manuscript instead applies different Euler--Maclaurin remainder orders,
\(O(Q^{-\sigma_0})\) and \(O(Q^{-1+\sigma_0})\), to the two approximants and
then treats those remainders as the leading values of the exact functions
near a common zero.  That substitution is invalid.  If
\[
\xi(s(Q),Q)=\xi(s(Q))+\varepsilon_Q(s(Q)),
\qquad
\xi(1-s(Q),Q)=\xi(1-s(Q))+\varepsilon_Q(1-s(Q)),
\]
then (189.1) makes the two exact main terms equal; it does not make either
one equal to its truncation error.  When the path is chosen so that
\(\xi(s(Q))\) tends to zero more slowly than one remainder, the other
remainder can dominate the corresponding approximant, but the resulting
ratio is a ratio of two *approximation errors*, not the exact ratio in
(189.2).  No theorem in the manuscript controls that ratio or transfers its
behavior to the exact function.

The claimed “same form” step therefore replaces an exact equality by an
unstated asymptotic equivalence.  The alleged equations (6) and (7) are not
simultaneously consequences of the definitions; equation (6) uses error
orders as exact values, while equation (7) uses the exact functional
equation.  The contradiction is generated by this category error, not by an
off-critical zero.

\[
\boxed{\text{\rm UNPROVED LOAD-BEARING CLAIM}}
\]

No complete unconditional proof of the manuscript's contradiction was
obtained, and no new unconditional RH proof progress was produced.  Its
finite truncation formulas and parameterized limit discussion do not
establish any statement about the location of the exact nontrivial zeros.
