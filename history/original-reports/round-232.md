## Round 232 — A/B result: the well-ordering/symmetry manuscript reverses the meaning of minimality

**Source inspected:** R. D. Rusin, *A Structural Proof of the Riemann
Hypothesis: Well-Ordering, Symmetry, and the Critical Gap Theorem*,
<https://rxiv.org/pdf/2509.0037v1.pdf>, pp. 2–4.

The manuscript's Theorem 4.1 is a valid order observation: if
\(\rho_0=\beta+i\gamma\) is the lexicographically first off-line zero and
\(\beta>1/2\), then its functional-equation partner
\(\phi(\rho_0)=1-\overline{\rho_0}\) has the same height and smaller real
part, contradicting minimality. Therefore a minimal off-line zero, if one
exists, must have \(\beta<1/2\), and its partner is later in the stated
order.

The proof of Theorem 5.1 then makes the decisive invalid inference. It says
that the later partner \(\phi(\rho_0)\) is also in the off-line set and hence
contradicts the minimality of \(\rho_0\). This is the reverse of the
definition of a minimum: minimality forbids an element strictly *before*
\(\rho_0\), not an element strictly after it. Theorem 4.1 has already shown
that the partner is after \(\rho_0\); that fact is compatible with, rather
than contradictory to, minimality.

The insufficiency is exhibited by the abstract finite configuration
\[
\{\,\tfrac14+i,\ \tfrac34+i,\ \tfrac14-i,\ \tfrac34-i\,\}.
\]
It is discrete, lies in the critical strip, has the required conjugation and
functional-equation symmetries, and has no boundary points. Its
lexicographically first upper-half-plane off-line point is
\(\tfrac14+i\), whose symmetric partner \(\tfrac34+i\) occurs later. Thus
all structural premises used by the claimed contradiction are compatible
with off-line points. The actual zeta function supplies additional analytic
structure, but no additional theorem is supplied in this manuscript that
would exclude this order pattern.

Consequently the manuscript proves only the one-sided placement statement
for a hypothetical first off-line zero. It does not prove RH and does not
produce a disproof.
