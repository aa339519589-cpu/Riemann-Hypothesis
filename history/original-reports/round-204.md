## Round 204 — A/B result: the semilocal manuscript discards the \(\epsilon_N\) shift

The primary semilocal theorem constructs its self-adjoint operator from
\[
T_{N,\lambda}=Q_{W,\lambda}^N-\epsilon_N I,
\]
where \(\epsilon_N\) is the smallest eigenvalue of
\(Q_{W,\lambda}^N\).  The definition of a smallest eigenvalue gives
\[
\langle T_{N,\lambda}v,v\rangle\ge0,
\]
but the original form satisfies the exact identity
\[
\langle Q_{W,\lambda}^Nv,v\rangle
=\langle T_{N,\lambda}v,v\rangle+\epsilon_N\|v\|^2.
\]
Consequently positivity of \(T_{N,\lambda}\) implies positivity of
\(Q_{W,\lambda}^N\) only after an independent proof that
\(\epsilon_N\ge0\).  Neither the cited primary theorem nor the manuscript
proves that sign.

The manuscript's claimed sum-of-squares formula is written for the
unshifted \(Q_{W,\lambda}^N\), but the cited primary Theorem 5.10 only
provides self-adjointness after the shift and under the simple-even
assumptions.  Reality of the resulting auxiliary spectral parameters
therefore cannot be substituted for positivity of the unshifted Weil form.
No exact trace or determinant identity in the cited theorem identifies
those auxiliary parameters with the zeros of \(\zeta\).

This is independent of the other omitted hypotheses and limit gaps.  The
finite-dimensional shifted form may have a real-spectrum construction while
the unshifted form and its arithmetic limit remain uncontrolled.  Hence the
manuscript's finite sum-of-squares step does not prove global Weil
positivity.
