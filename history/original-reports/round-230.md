## Round 230 — A/B result: Zhou--Zhou's claimed off-line-zero disproof depends on an unproved permanent upper bound

**Source inspected:** E. Zhou and M. Zhou, *On Existence of Nontrivial Zeros
of the Riemann Zeta Function off the Critical Line*, Zenodo record
<https://zenodo.org/records/21964450>, PDF pp. 1–12 and Appendix B, pp. 14–15.

The manuscript does not exhibit a specific complex number \(\rho\) with
\(\zeta(\rho)=0\) and \(\operatorname{Re}\rho\ne1/2\). Its proposed
contradiction assumes RH in equation (17), rewrites the reciprocal sum of all
critical-line zeros as \(S_{\rm on}\), and attempts to construct an analytic
upper bound \(S_V\) for that infinite sum. The conclusion requires the bound
to hold permanently after a finite index.

That permanence is not proved. Theorem 4 in Appendix B is the stated result
that \(S_V\) has a limit and decreases globally (up to claimed fluctuations),
but its entire proof is: “A complete proof of this Theorem is lengthy and will
be presented in a subsequent version.” The main text nevertheless uses this
unproved theorem to infer from a comparison with the first 2,001,052 tabulated
critical-line zeros that the inequality persists for all later indices. A
finite numerical comparison cannot establish an infinite tail inequality
without the missing theorem.

There is an additional unsupported grouping step. The argument partitions the
unknown zero gaps into groups and asserts that the relevant gaps can be made
greater than 1 by deleting and reindexing zeros, while retaining every zero in
the sum. The Riemann–von Mangoldt mean-gap asymptotic only says that the
average gap tends to zero; it does not prove the asserted global group
structure or the recurrence inequalities for the reindexed series. The paper
also treats the conditional reciprocal sum as if the unknown critical-line
zero sequence were fully available, while its numerical part contains only a
finite prefix.

Thus the contradiction rests on an explicitly unproved infinite-tail
monotonicity theorem and finite numerical data. No explicit off-line zero is
verified, so the claimed disproof is not established.
