## Round 237 — A/B result: finite spectral capacity does not imply the claimed zero-location constraint

**Source inspected:** M. I. Aksman, *Thermodynamic Proof of the Riemann
Hypothesis: Kinematic Stability of the Finite Arithmetic Vacuum*, Zenodo
record <https://zenodo.org/records/21728379>, PDF pp. 1–6.

The manuscript's finite-capacity functional
\[
C(N)=\sum_{k=0}^{N}(\lambda_{\max}-\lambda_k)
\]
is a finite scalar for a chosen finite model, but this does not imply that
every solution of a dynamical equation on that model is bounded. Finite
dimensional linear systems can have exponentially growing modes, and
nonlinear finite-dimensional systems can have unbounded trajectories. A
separate dynamical theorem relating the proposed capacity to a uniform
stability bound is required and is not given.

The claimed mapping from an off-line zero
\(s=\sigma+i\rho_n\) to a physical mode \(e^{\pm\epsilon t}e^{i\rho_n t}\)
is also asserted rather than derived. The source cites a turbulence
framework but supplies no equation, solution space, transform identity, or
boundary condition proving that every zeta zero produces such a mode in the
DSM-861 dynamics. In particular, functional-equation symmetry only pairs
zeros; it does not by itself create an unstable physical degree of freedom.

The contradiction in Theorem 5.1 therefore assumes both the finite-vacuum
axiom and the exact zero-to-mode correspondence, then assumes that
“structural rupture” is impossible. None of these implications is a
mathematical consequence of the standard analytic properties of \(\zeta\).
The preceding capacity monotonicity statement does not supply them, and the
physical claims about invariant mass and gravity are unrelated to the
location of the zeta divisor.

Thus the manuscript offers a physical analogy and a conditional argument:
if the proposed vacuum model were stable, finite, and exactly coupled to the
zeta zeros in the stated way, then the asserted growth would be forbidden.
It does not prove those premises, so it does not prove RH or disprove it.
