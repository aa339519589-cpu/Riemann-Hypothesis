## Round 217 — A/B result: Aksman's revised vorton--FUP manuscript still contradicts its own positive-core geometry and lacks a valid resonance limit

**Source inspected:** Mikhail I. Aksman, *The Spectral Resolution of the
Riemann Hypothesis: Automorphic Scattering, Fractal Uncertainty, and Coercive
Vorton Geometry*, August 2026, Zenodo record
<https://zenodo.org/records/22136739>, PDF pp. 1--6.

The positive-core regularization is incompatible with Lemma 3.2.  The
manuscript defines \(K_a\) using filaments with a strictly positive radius
\(a>0\), then claims that every point of \(K_a\) and every scale \(r\ge h\)
has a complementary ball \(B(y,\nu r)\subset B(x,r)\setminus K_a\), with
\(\nu\) independent of \(a\).  An interior point of a positive-radius
tubular neighborhood has a sufficiently small ball contained in \(K_a\), so
the asserted complementary ball does not exist.  If the displayed
nonlocal mollifier is interpreted literally, it is positive throughout the
ambient space, making its support even less porous.  The accompanying
Ahlfors--David estimate with exponent \(\delta<3\) cannot hold uniformly down
to arbitrarily small scales for a three-dimensional thickening either.

Consequently the hypotheses needed for the FUP exponent in Theorem 3.3 are
not established.  The later transfer is another independent gap: strong
resolvent convergence of \(P_a\) to \(P\), even if proved, does not by itself
give uniform convergence of meromorphic resolvents or convergence of their
poles on compact subsets.  The manuscript supplies no common domain theorem,
uniform meromorphic bounds, or pole-stability result that would justify
Theorem 4.2's Phragmén--Lindelöf conclusion.

The claimed zero exclusion therefore remains conditional on the missing
porosity/FUP and resonance-transfer theorems.  The appendix's finite numerical
checks of a \(\Gamma_0(163)\) scattering matrix do not establish an
infinite-dimensional resonance-free half-plane or an exact bijection with all
zeros of \(\zeta\).

Thus this revised manuscript produces no new unconditional load-bearing
theorem for RH.
