## Round 264 — B-result: the Fredholm--scattering candidate does not construct the claimed operator or causality bridge

Source inspected: Mikhail I. Aksman, *A Fredholm--Scattering Program for
the Hilbert--Pólya Operator*, Zenodo record
[22149909](https://zenodo.org/records/22149909), PDF SHA-256
5fc4cbe248aa6fad3660d8210fe18cb18b29d2931849deb50b690df885c9d503.

The four-page manuscript asserts a self-adjoint operator on a finite-volume
hyperbolic orbifold, a scattering determinant related to the completed zeta
function, and a regularized Wigner-delay lower bound. It does not specify
the group or orbifold, the quadratic-form domains, boundary conditions,
Mosco-limit construction, scattering matrix, determinant normalization, or
the trace/determinant identity. Thus the claimed spectral realization of
\(\xi(s)\) is an assertion, not a proved theorem.

The displayed Hardy-coercivity calculation only shows that a resonance
state is not an \(L^2\) eigenvector. It does not constrain the location of a
resonance. The decisive causality step simply asserts
\[
\tau_{\mathrm{reg}}(t)\ge-2D_{\max}
\]
after subtracting an unspecified cusp delay. No theorem is supplied that
Wigner's finite-range causality bound survives this regularization or
applies to the meromorphic automorphic scattering continuation.

Even accepting the formal Blaschke calculation, the manuscript never proves
that an arbitrary off-line zeta zero produces the stated isolated
scattering pole and symmetric pair, that all other scattering factors make
no compensating contribution to the regularized delay, or that a truncation
can simultaneously preserve the claimed scattering problem and satisfy
\(D_{\max}<4\). The conclusion
\(\tau_{\mathrm{pole}}(t_0)\le-8<-2D_{\max}\) therefore rests on
unproved geometric and analytic premises. There is no unconditional
zero-exclusion theorem and no RH proof.
