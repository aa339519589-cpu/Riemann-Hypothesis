## Round 233 — A/B result: the finite-field realization relocates RH to an unproved completeness condition

**Source inspected:** Yosef Akhtman, *A Finite Field Realisation of the
Riemann Hypothesis*, Preprints.org, Version 2,
<https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202606.0768>, especially the abstract
and Sections 7--10.

The finite-shell statements are not the RH. The construction can place the
modes of a finite-shell operator on a self-dual line by construction, and a
finite numerical reconstruction can match known heights. These facts concern
the modes produced by the construction. They do not identify those modes
with every zero of the classical \(\zeta\)-function.

The paper's Lemma 7.4 explicitly states that faithfulness of the de-framing,
completeness of the on-line spectrum for the prime vector, absence of
off-line zeros, and RH are equivalent. This is a criterion/reformulation,
not a proof of any one of the four statements. The subsequent argument
never proves the completeness clause. In fact, the paper explicitly says
that exact completeness is the unresolved “zero-leak closure” and that the
bounded clause remains unverified beyond the numerically checked height.

The proposed finite closure does not repair this. The manuscript treats the
finitude of the totality and frame-covariance of the height chart as the two
premises that close the quantifier, while also stating that the finitude
premise is under review. Even if that external premise were granted, it
would only make the relevant framework finite; it would not prove that the
classical zeta divisor is exhausted by the finite shell's on-line modes, nor
exclude a finite-height off-line residual. The manuscript expressly retains
that residual as the unclosed completeness object.

The operator step has the same missing identification. The bare dilation
generator \(\widehat H=-i(x\partial_x+1/2)\) on the multiplicative line has
continuous spectrum. A finite discretization or a real-symmetric Jacobi
matrix necessarily has real spectrum, but self-adjointness alone does not
show that its eigenvalues are the zeta zero ordinates. The source itself
states that self-adjointness is easy and that faithfulness—spectrum equal to
the zeros rather than a height-matched image—is the hard unresolved point.
Numerical agreement through a finite height cannot establish that
identification for all zeros.

Therefore the manuscript supplies finite algebraic constructions, a
reformulation of RH as completeness, and finite numerical consistency. It
does not supply the load-bearing completeness theorem or an unconditional
zero-location proof.
