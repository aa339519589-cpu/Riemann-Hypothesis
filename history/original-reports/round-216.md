## Round 216 — A/B result: Aksman's Heegner-orbifold manuscript assumes finite spectral capacity and an invalid prime/geodesic correspondence

**Source inspected:** Mikhail I. Aksman, *A Fredholm--Scattering
Formulation of the Hilbert--Pólya Program: Bounding the Siegel Zero via
Hydrodynamic Resonance on the Heegner Orbifold*, August 2026, Zenodo record
<https://zenodo.org/records/22115872>, PDF pp. 1--8.

The central “Finite Spectral Capacity Axiom” simply postulates that the
continuous Hilbert space of the non-compact surface \(X_{163}) is isomorphic
to an 861-dimensional graph space.  An isometric embedding of a finite graph
space into an infinite-dimensional automorphic \(L^2\) space does not make the
ambient space finite-dimensional, remove its cusp, or truncate its continuous
spectrum.  No theorem establishes the asserted equality of spaces or a
projection that preserves the Laplace--Beltrami scattering determinant.

The arithmetic-geometric bridge is also false as stated.  The primitive
hyperbolic conjugacy classes of \(\Gamma_0(163)\) are not in bijection with
ordinary prime numbers, and their norms are not all primes.  The Selberg trace
formula therefore cannot be reduced by the asserted identity
\(N(\gamma_0)=p\) to the ordinary von Mangoldt prime-power sum.  The class
number-one property of \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-163})\) supplies no such bijection
and no theorem identifying this modular curve's length spectrum with the
ordinary zeta Euler product.

The finite determinant itself cannot represent all non-trivial zeta zeros: a
determinant of an 861-dimensional operator is a polynomial with at most 861
eigenvalue roots, whereas \(\xi(s)\) has infinitely many non-trivial zeros.
The manuscript avoids this mismatch by speaking only of a “represented
spectral sector”, which is insufficient for RH.

Finally, the alleged inverse correspondence is circular.  Equation (17) maps
a generic zero to \(\lambda=\rho(1-\rho)\), but the next displayed
calculation begins, “Assuming a zeta zero assumes the critical form
\(\rho=1/2+i\gamma\)”.  That assumption is precisely the conclusion needed
to prove.  The preceding hydrodynamic prohibition of a Siegel zero is a
physical analogy, not a mathematical exclusion theorem for zeta zeros.

Thus the finite-capacity axiom, prime/geodesic identification, and final
spectral map do not establish an unconditional RH proof.
