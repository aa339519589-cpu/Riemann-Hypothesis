## Round 81 — the Shimizu determinant route does not establish the scalar-to-trace bridge

I checked the current Shimizu record [*Proof of the Riemann Hypothesis*, version 2.4](https://zenodo.org/records/21108581), whose own record carries a disclaimer that the working draft contains fatal errors, together with the version-8 public abstract ([latest manuscript page](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202505.2110)). The self-adjoint Hilbert--Schmidt construction alone is not enough. The load-bearing step is the claimed identification of the ordinary scalar finite-part coefficients with powers of the constructed operator.

### I. What is obtained without the disputed bridge

The manuscript constructs a self-adjoint Hilbert--Schmidt operator \(K\) and therefore an intrinsic regularized determinant
\[
D_K(z)=\det\nolimits_2(I+zK).
\tag{81.1}
\]
For a self-adjoint \(K\), the determinant factor has zeros generated by real eigenvalues. This is a valid spectral fact about \(D_K\), but it says nothing about the zeros of \(\xi\) until the independent identity
\[
e^{a+b(s-\frac12)}
D_K\!\left(i(s-\tfrac12)\right)=\xi(s)
\tag{81.2}
\]
has been proved.

### II. Lemma 6.129 changes a linear scalar pairing into a nonlinear matrix trace

In the downloadable manuscript, the scalar finite-window functional
\(\mu^{\mathrm{fp,sc}}_{R,M}\) is an ordinary linear functional on scalar
finite-window tests. The manuscript then defines a separate cyclic tensor lift
by
\[
\left\langle\mu^{(\ell)}_{R,M},
\Omega_1\mathbin{\odot_M}\cdots\mathbin{\odot_M}\Omega_\ell\right\rangle
:=
\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}
\left\langle\mu^{\mathrm{fp,sc}}_{R,M},\Omega_j\right\rangle.
\tag{81.3}
\]
For matrix-coefficient tests, the preceding finite readout identity gives
\[
c_{ab}
=\left\langle\mu^{\mathrm{fp,sc}}_{R,M},\Omega_{ab}\right\rangle
=\left\langle K_M e_b,e_a\right\rangle.
\tag{81.4}
\]
Consequently, (81.3) does give the cyclic tensor contraction
\[
\sum_{a_1,\ldots,a_\ell}
c_{a_1a_2}c_{a_2a_3}\cdots c_{a_\ell a_1}
=\operatorname{tr}(K_{M,N}^{\ell}).
\tag{81.5}
\]
But (81.5) is a statement about the newly defined tensor functional. It does
not follow from linearity that the ordinary scalar coefficient satisfies
\[
\left\langle\mu^{\mathrm{fp,sc}}_{R,M},
\Psi^{\mathrm{sc}}_{M,\ell}\right\rangle
=\operatorname{Tr}(K_M^\ell).
\tag{81.6}
\]
The left side of (81.6) is linear in the scalar test
\(\Psi^{\mathrm{sc}}_{M,\ell}\), whereas the right side is an \(\ell\)-th
degree polynomial in the scalar matrix entries (81.4). To justify (81.6), one
would need an additional theorem identifying the ordinary scalar test with the
specific \(\ell\)-fold tensorized readout and proving the corresponding
multiplicativity or diagonal pullback. The manuscript explicitly keeps the
scalar and cyclic codomains distinct and says that the scalar functional is
not multiplicative on ordinary products; it supplies no such identification.

Thus the first equality asserted in Lemma 6.129 is the missing step, while the
second equality is obtained from the tensor-lift definition. The subsequent
finite-part-to-determinant trace theorem and the local identity of logarithmic
derivatives depend on (81.6). Without it, the constructed self-adjoint
determinant is an auxiliary entire function, not an independently identified
copy of \(\xi\).

**Established:** a self-adjoint Hilbert--Schmidt operator has a real spectral
determinant, and the separately defined cyclic tensor contraction produces
finite matrix trace powers.

**Failure / missing step:** the ordinary scalar finite-part coefficient is not
shown to equal the cyclic tensor contraction. The source record itself flags
the working draft as containing fatal proof errors.

**Remaining load-bearing statement:** prove a non-circular scalar-to-tensor
pullback/multiplicativity theorem that yields (81.6), then prove the complete
normalization and analytic identification (81.2). The current construction
does not close that bridge.

**RH status:** no unconditional proof has been produced.
