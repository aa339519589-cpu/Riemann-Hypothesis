## Round 84 — Abe's claimed zero-free region loses a tangent-intercept factor

I audited the current Version 26 of Abe's [*A rigorous proof of the
Riemann hypothesis*](https://www.cambridge.org/engage/coe/article-details/6a52f0d3810b9dcc82011b00).
The page identifies it as a working paper and says it has not been
peer-reviewed, but the decisive issue is an explicit algebraic error in the
proof of its Theorem 1.

Set
\[
d:=\frac{1}{2\alpha},\qquad
q:=\sqrt{\frac{1}{\alpha}R(s)-R(s)^2}.
\]
On the putative zero locus, equation (5.1) gives \(I(s)^2=q^2\); in the
positive branch used by the manuscript, \(I(s)=q\). The tangent line to the
second circle \(C_2\) is displayed in the paper as
\[
Y=\frac{d-R(s)}{q}\bigl(X-R(s)\bigr)+I(s).
\tag{84.1}
\]
Putting \(Y=0\) in (84.1) gives its \(x\)-intercept
\[
X_B
=R(s)-\frac{I(s)q}{d-R(s)}
=R(s)-\frac{I(s)^2}{d-R(s)}
=R(s)-\frac{\frac1\alpha R(s)-R(s)^2}{\frac1{2\alpha}-R(s)}.
\tag{84.2}
\]
However, the manuscript's equation (5.5) uses
\[
X_B^{\mathrm{paper}}
=R(s)-\frac{q}{d-R(s)},
\tag{84.3}
\]
which contains \(q\) rather than \(I(s)q=q^2\). Thus (5.5) is not the
intersection of the displayed tangent line with the \(x\)-axis. Equation
(5.6), and therefore the claimed zero-free Theorem 1, is derived from this
incorrect coordinate.

The size of the resulting false contradiction makes the error transparent.
With \(\delta=2\sigma-1>0\), the paper's own asymptotics give
\[
R(s)=t^{-2}+O(t^{-4}),\qquad
I(s)=\delta\,t^{-3}+O(t^{-4}),\qquad
d-R(s)=-\frac12t^{-2}+O(t^{-4}).
\tag{84.4}
\]
The erroneous right side in (84.3) is then
\[
R(s)-\frac{I(s)}{d-R(s)}
=2\delta\,t^{-1}+O(t^{-2}),
\tag{84.5}
\]
whereas the left tangent-distance expression is \(R(s)+O(t^{-2})\). The
paper's contradiction is therefore generated by replacing \(I^2\) with \(I\).
With the correct intercept (84.2), the right side is instead
\[
R(s)-\frac{I(s)^2}{d-R(s)}
=t^{-2}+O(t^{-4}),
\tag{84.6}
\]
which has the same leading order as the left side and supplies no zero
exclusion. The corrected geometric relation is an identity-level consequence
of the two circle equations, not the claimed asymptotic contradiction.

Indeed, write \(c=1/(2\beta)\) and \(d=1/(2\alpha)\). The two circle
equations imply
\[
R^2+I^2=2cI=2dR,
\]
so, on the positive branch, \((R,I)=\kappa(c,d)\) with
\(\kappa(c^2+d^2)=2cd\). The tangent-distance formulas reduce to
\[
PA=\frac{Rc}{c-I},\qquad
OB=\frac{dR}{R-d}
\]
in the large-\(t\) sign regime \(R>d\). Their equality is then exactly
\[
c(R-d)=d(c-I)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\kappa(c^2+d^2)=2cd.
\]
Thus the corrected equation contributes no independent condition on a zero.

**Verdict:** the claimed Theorem 1 is not proved; the final argument for RH
collapses at equation (5.5). The source's Version 26 label does not repair the
missing factor.

**RH status:** no unconditional proof has been produced.
