## Round 224 — A/B result: Yang--Yang's density-one theorem does not imply the Riemann Hypothesis

**Source inspected:** Hongyi Yang and Shihua Yang, *Almost all zeros of the
Riemann zeta function are simple and on the critical line*, Zenodo record
<https://zenodo.org/records/22065921>, PDF pp. 1–6, 38–45.

The paper's headline Theorem 12.7 claims only

\[
\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{N_0^s(T,2T)}{N(T,2T)}=1,
\qquad
\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{N_d(T,2T)}{N(T,2T)}=1.
\]

These are density-one statements. They permit an exceptional set of
off-critical or multiple zeros whose relative density tends to zero, and
therefore do not imply that the exceptional set is empty. The paper itself
states in Remark 12.9 that it does not assert that all zeros are simple or lie
on the line; its introductory discussion likewise explicitly says that the
proportion statements are insensitive to \(o(N(T))\) off-line zeros.

The logical gap is independent of the moment calculations: for any sequence
of zero counts with \(N_0^s(T,2T)/N(T,2T)\to1\), one can still have at least
one off-line zero in infinitely many height intervals, or finitely many such
zeros. Density one rules out only a positive-proportion obstruction. It does
not establish the universal statement required by RH. Numerical checks and
finite exact moment certificates cannot change this quantifier gap.

Moreover, the manuscript grades its analytic chain as a certified candidate
pending external review and leaves it unformalized, while its own scope
statement says that nothing in the paper bears on RH itself. Thus, even if the
density-one theorem were accepted at its stated grade, it would still be a
strictly weaker result and supplies no unconditional RH proof or disproof.
