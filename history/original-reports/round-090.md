## Round 90 — Burnol's completeness theorem concerns all zeros, not the critical subset needed by Bombieri

Burnol's journal article [*Two complete and minimal systems associated with the zeros of the Riemann zeta function*](https://jtnb.centre-mersenne.org/articles/10.5802/jtnb.434/) proves a precise Hilbert-space theorem for the evaluators attached to the full multiset of nontrivial zeros, with multiplicities. In the extended Sonine space \(\mathcal L_a\), the full zero system is minimal exactly at the relevant threshold and complete for \(a>1\); the associated Mellin functions \(\zeta(s)/(s-\rho)^\ell\) form a complete system in the corresponding dense subspace. These are genuine completeness/minimality results.

They do not imply the statement needed in the present Bombieri route. Let
\[
\Gamma_{\mathrm{all}}
=\Gamma_C\sqcup\Gamma_{\mathrm{off}},
\]
where \(\Gamma_C\) denotes critical-line ordinates and \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{off}}\) denotes the off-line data. Burnol's theorem gives a closure statement for the span generated by \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{all}}\). The implication
\[
\overline{\operatorname{span}}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{all}})=H
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\overline{\operatorname{span}}(\Gamma_C)=H
\tag{90.1}
\]
is false for general Hilbert systems: deleting a subfamily can destroy completeness. Establishing (90.1) for the actual zeta zeros without assuming that \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{off}}\) is empty is itself an independent arithmetic theorem, and the cited result does not establish it.

There is a second, stronger mismatch. Bombieri's surviving equation is not merely a density statement; it asks for a prescribed function to have an exact expansion with coefficients in a weighted \(\ell^2\) domain:
\[
g_{\mathrm{off},I}
=-\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_C}
\frac{w_\gamma}{\frac14+\gamma^2}e^{-i\gamma u},
\qquad w\in\ell^2(\Gamma_C).
\tag{90.2}
\]
Completeness supplies only approximating finite sums. Burnol's dual systems live in Mellin/Sonine spaces and do not provide the coefficient estimate or range-membership assertion in (90.2). The compactness argument in Round 85 shows why this upgrade is nontrivial: a dense nonclosed range can approximate the target while failing to contain it.

Thus a route that cites Burnol and says “the zero exponentials are complete” has silently changed the indexed set from all zeros to critical zeros and changed closure into exact weighted synthesis. Both changes are load-bearing.

**Established:** full-zero completeness/minimality theorems in the Sonine/Mellin setting; these are valid analytic tools.

**Failure / missing step:** no RH-independent theorem in the cited source transfers completeness to the critical-line subset or proves the weighted range relation (90.2).

**Route decision:** use Burnol's full-system theorem only as a structural benchmark. The selected route remains the search for a direct arithmetic range-exclusion or full Weil-positivity theorem, neither of which follows from Sonine completeness.

**RH status:** no unconditional proof has been produced.
