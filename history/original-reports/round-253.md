## Round 253 — A/B result: Stone's hardware-stability manuscript has no mathematical bridge to the zeta zeros

Source audited: Travis Raymond-Charlie Stone, *Mechanical Synthesis and Stability
Resolution of the Riemann Hypothesis via Successional Wave Architecture*,
[Zenodo record 19054576](https://zenodo.org/records/19054576),
main PDF SHA-256 3b55151dcd36f73005f7aa2bc7bad0dced1cb87b03c0b76d136fcca864cc82c9.
The four-page document defines a 900-unit virtual transistor model and reports
1,000 finite test cycles.

The alleged implication is not a statement in the analytic theory of
\(\zeta\). The field variable \(F\) is calibrated at the outset to the
number \(0.5\), and the reported coherence statistic only measures overlap
of the model's three internally defined banks. No map is defined from a
non-trivial zero \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) to a state of the manifold, and no
theorem relates the finite recurrence, the quantity \(M\cdot T\cdot F=S\),
or the absence of a hardware bifurcation to \(\beta=0.5\). The conclusion
therefore restates the calibration choice rather than deriving a property of
the Riemann zeta function.

The finite experiment cannot establish a universal statement about the
infinitely many zeros. In particular, observing stability in 1,000 cycles
does not prove a zero-free region, does not produce an exact off-critical
counterexample, and does not imply the Weil, Li, or explicit-formula
criterion for RH. The document supplies no mathematical definition of the
reported quantities that would permit such an implication or independent
verification.

Thus the manuscript yields neither a proof nor a disproof of RH; its
empirical stability observation has no load-bearing mathematical connection
to the zero-location assertion.
