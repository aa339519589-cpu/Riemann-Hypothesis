## Round 212 — A/B result: Aksman's vorton--FUP chain fails at uniform porosity and at the resonance-strip transfer

**Source inspected:** Mikhail I. Aksman, *The Riemann Hypothesis:
Automorphic Scattering, Fractal Uncertainty, and Coercive Vorton Geometry*,
PDF pp. 1--5, Zenodo record
<https://zenodo.org/records/22063440>.

The manuscript fixes a strictly positive core radius \(a>0\) and defines
\(K_a\) as a finite-core tubular regularization of the geodesic trapped set.
Lemma 2.2 nevertheless asserts that for every \(x\in K_a\), every
semiclassical scale down to \(h\), and a constant \(\nu>0\) independent of
\(a\), there is a ball \(B(y,\nu r)\subset B(x,r)\setminus K_a\).  A
positive-radius tubular neighborhood has interior.  Choose an interior point
\(x\) and \(r>0\) smaller than its distance to the boundary; then
\(B(x,r)\subset K_a\), so no nonempty ball \(B(y,\nu r)\) can lie in its
complement.  Thus the stated all-scales porosity lemma is incompatible with
the manuscript's own \(a>0\) regularization.

Theorem 1.1 also infers a uniform lower bound
\(\beta_a\ge\beta_0>0\) from structural stability, but it supplies no
defined perturbed Hamiltonian flow, convergence topology, or theorem showing
that the FUP exponent is preserved under this finite-core replacement.
Consequently Theorem 2.3 and Proposition 3.1 have no established geometric
hypothesis.

There is a separate parameter gap in the zero-transfer step.  Proposition
3.1 gives only a resolvent-free region
\[
\Re(s)>\frac12-\varepsilon(\beta)
\qquad\text{for some }\varepsilon(\beta)>0.
\]
Theorem 3.2 immediately treats this as the region
\(\Re(s)>\frac14\), which would require the unproved quantitative bound
\(\varepsilon(\beta)\ge\frac14\).  Without that bound, the result excludes
only the corresponding smaller portion of the right half-plane and cannot
exclude every zero with \(\Re(\rho)>\frac12\) under the map \(\rho=2s\).
The functional equation cannot supply the missing quantitative width.

Hence the vorton--FUP chain does not prove an unconditional resonance gap
large enough to imply RH.
