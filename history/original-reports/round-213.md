## Round 213 — A/B result: Kulik's prime-field closure remains an unproved global frame/determinant condition

**Source inspected:** Dean A. Kulik, *The Prime-Field Closure Functional: An
Operator-Theoretic Resolution of the Riemann Hypothesis*, Ver Mark 9, PDF
pp. 10--17, Zenodo record
<https://zenodo.org/records/20361705>.

The manuscript's local detector discussion does not close the global
implication.  It states that “No Cross-Phase Escape” requires the
seam-orthogonal defect packets to form a Riesz sequence, or to have a uniform
lower frame bound, in a specified Hilbert space.  But the proof-status table
itself labels the Frame Trap—the global functional satisfying all four
obligations—as the “live mathematical bolt / specific operator required”.  No
Hilbert space, packet family, lower-frame constant, or proof of the
ordinary-prime embedding is supplied.

The later Buchstab/Laguerre--Mellin discussion asserts that such a basis
“guarantees” the Riesz condition without proving the required frame
inequality.  The text also identifies the exact target as an unproved
spectral-radius bound (equivalently Fredholm-determinant nonvanishing) for all
parameters in the critical-strip/right-half-plane range.  The Bornemann/Nyström
section supplies only finite numerical approximations and calls them a step
toward a globally secure proof; finite determinant values do not establish
nonvanishing of the infinite determinant.

Finally, the “ontological closure” section treats the prime field's being a
fixed table of integers as implying that the global detector equals zero.  No
mathematical functional identity proves that assertion.  It is the desired
closure condition, not a consequence of the preceding definitions.

Thus the source supplies neither the global lower-frame theorem nor the
determinant nonvanishing/zero-exclusion theorem needed to deduce RH.
