## Round 221 — A/B result: Döring's projection synopsis confuses real zero ordinates with the real parts of zeros

**Source inspected:** Holger Döring, *A short Synopsis of possible Proof of
the Riemann Hypothesis via Self-Adjoint Projection Operators in \(L^2(\mathbb
R)\)*, August 2026, Zenodo record
<https://zenodo.org/records/22012452>, PDF pp. 1--8.

The central “spectral reality” lemma does not imply the stated conclusion.
For every non-trivial zero one can write
\(\rho=\sigma+i\gamma\) with \(\gamma\in\mathbb R\), regardless of whether
\(\sigma=1/2\).  A self-adjoint operator whose eigenvalue is identified only
with this already-real ordinate \(\gamma\) gives no information about
\(\sigma\).  The sentence that symmetry around the critical line then forces
\(\operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2\) is a non sequitur.

The manuscript's projection construction only removes the span of explicitly
chosen Gaussian prime wavefunctions from \(L^2(\mathbb R)\).  Strong convergence
of the finite orthogonal projections can establish a projection onto the
orthogonal complement, but it does not construct the claimed Hamiltonian
\(H_\zeta\), identify its spectrum with all zeta zeros, or prove a trace/explicit
formula connecting the two.  No such zero-to-spectrum theorem is supplied;
the correspondence is assumed in the abstract and then used in the
conclusion.

Thus self-adjointness of the proposed projection framework does not exclude
off-critical real parts, and the synopsis contains no complete unconditional
RH proof.
